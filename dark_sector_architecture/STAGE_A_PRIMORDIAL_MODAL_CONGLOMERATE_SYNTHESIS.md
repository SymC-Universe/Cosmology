## 1. Scalar lane

## 2. Modal lane

The primordial perturbation problem has a nontrivial regular mode basis.

For the standard photon-baryon-neutrino-CDM system, Bucher, Moodley & Turok identify the regular scalar modes:

- adiabatic growing mode;
- baryon density isocurvature mode;
- CDM density isocurvature mode;
- neutrino density isocurvature mode;
- neutrino velocity isocurvature mode.

The decaying adiabatic solution is singular at early times and is not part of the regular five-mode initial-condition basis.

This means the primordial state is intrinsically modal. A single scalar such as (zeta) describes only one projection/direction of that larger state space.

## 3. Dynamical modal reorganization through horizon entry

The adiabatic primordial state does not remain a single undifferentiated behavior after horizon entry.

Different physical sectors realize that shared initial organization differently:

- photon-baryon perturbations enter an acoustic branch because radiation pressure supplies restoring dynamics;
- neutrino free streaming supplies a distinct anisotropic-stress response and phase influence;
- a pressureless component supplies a non-acoustic gravitational-growth/collapse branch in standard cosmology.

Nadkarni-Ghosh & Refregier independently show that a reduced Einstein-Boltzmann generator develops changing eigenstructure across the (k-a) plane, including transitions between real and complex eigenvalues and an adiabatic/quasi-autonomous regime.

The modal architecture is therefore dynamic and scale dependent.

## 8. Current limits

The literature currently indicates:

- standard GR has no freely propagating scalar metric degree of freedom in vacuum; scalar gravitational potentials are constrained by the matter/energy perturbations;
- standard photon-baryon dynamics are acoustic before recombination;
- the observed CMB strongly favors an adiabatic-dominated initial modal organization;
- standard cosmology obtains the persistent non-acoustic scalar gravitational function from the pressureless CDM sector.

None of these facts proves that a broader non-substance conglomerate cannot realize the required function through nonlinear, correlation, nonlocal, or cross-sector organization.

They do define the function such an architecture would have to reproduce.
