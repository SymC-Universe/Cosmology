# Stage A Significant Finding: Early-Universe Constraint

Status: P0-D foundation conglomeration  
Disposition: Material scope-changing result

The admitted GR averaging, macroscopic-gravity, and perturbative-backreaction literature does not presently provide a quantitatively sufficient pre-recombination gravitational contribution that behaves like the cold-dark-matter role required by the CMB.

This does not rule out a broader architecture hypothesis. It does rule out treating the currently conglomerated late-time/structure-generated backreaction terms as though they already explain the full dark-matter function across cosmic history.

## Evidence

Li & Schwarz find that perturbative kinematical backreaction becomes relatively more important only as the Universe evolves. In their dust treatment,

[

qquad
ho_{mathcal D}propto a^{-3},
]

so (Q_{mathcal D}/ho_{mathcal D}propto a^2), making the effect most negligible at early times.

Brown, Robbers & Behrend find that radiation-era effective backreaction behaves approximately as

[
ho_{m eff}propto a^{-4},
qquad
w_{m eff}ightarrow 1/3,
]

with a magnitude around the (10^{-8}) level near recombination in their calculation. This is radiation-like rather than pressureless-matter-like.

Paranjape's covariant macroscopic-gravity calculation finds radiation-era correlation scalars roughly (10^{-13}) to (10^{-7}) relative to (H^2), remaining negligible compared with the background. In the matter era the dominant term tends toward curvature-like scaling.

Coley, Pelavas & Zalaletdinov obtain a macroscopic-gravity correlation contribution scaling as (a^{-2}) with (w=-1/3), again curvature-like rather than CDM-like.

Planck independently constrains the baryon density while the acoustic peaks and lensing require substantially stronger gravitational potentials in the standard model than baryons alone supply.

## Residual fork

**Route P: Partial architecture.** The architecture may explain or reorganize only some late-time phenomena conventionally attributed to DM and/or DE while an independent early gravitational component remains required.

**Route I: Primordial/inherited architecture.** The full no-dark-substance hypothesis remains viable only if Stage A identifies a distinct primordial architectural carrier active before recombination that supplies the required non-oscillating gravitational potentials without violating CMB/BBN constraints.

A primordial carrier cannot be obtained by extrapolating late-time backreaction. It requires its own native derivation, inheritance map, and observational consequences.

## Current claim ceiling

Supported: two-way multiscale GR architectures exist; shear and expansion variance can have opposite functional roles in averaged GR; curvature/backreaction can become important in nonlinear late-time domains; formal history dependence can arise under coarse graining.

Not supported: complete dark-matter replacement; a pre-recombination CDM-like role from known backreaction; one demonstrated architecture spanning CMB to late acceleration; SymC inheritance as the mechanism connecting those eras.

The next Stage A decision is whether a credible primordial/inherited carrier already exists in the literature. If none exists, the full no-dark-substance hypothesis must narrow before preregistration.
